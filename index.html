About Me

I am a theoretical astrophysicist and cosmologist studying the first stars and galaxies through large-scale semi-numeric simulations of Cosmic Dawn. I develop theoretical simulations of the cosmological 21-cm signal in Python/Cython, aiming to predict the 21-cm global signal, power spectrum, and their temporal evolution in the first billion years of cosmic history. I test both standard and exotic cosmological scenarios such as ultra-light (fuzzy) dark matter and primordial black holes.

I obtained my Bachelor's Degree in Astrophysics from Ohio University in December 2017. I then earned my PhD from the University of Toledo in July 2025. I am currently a post-doctoral researcher in the astrophysics department of Stellenbosch University.

Research

My research focuses on Cosmic Dawn, the era in which the first stars and galaxies began forming and illuminating the universe. These first sources kicked off the Epoch of Reionization, the last great phase transition in cosmic history which saw the intergalactic medium evolve from neutral Hydrogen and Helium gas into the ionized plasma we see today. My work focuses on simulating the rise of these first stars and galaxies as a means of constraining the cosmological 21-cm signal arising from neutral hydrogen gas. Because this signal is solely produced by neutral atomic hydrogen, it traces the ionization history of the cosmos and allows us to determine the dominant processes and astrophysical processes that resulted in our modern universe.
